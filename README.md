# Nairobi Tour

A Nairobi remix of [Slop New York](https://github.com/mrdoob/slop-newyork): the CBD at 1:1 on its real terrain, built with [three.js](https://threejs.org/).

## Run it

The page needs a local web server because it loads ES modules:

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8080
```

Then open http://localhost:8080. While editing, `python3 tools/serve-nocache.py 8081` serves the same files with caching off, so changed modules load on every reload.

Controls: **1–4** switch between walking, driving, cycling and the helicopter. **W**/**S** go and brake, **A**/**D** turn, **Space** jumps on foot and climbs in the helicopter, **Q**/**C** descend. Drag to look around the player and scroll to zoom. **V** steps through the cameras (**5–0** pick them directly): chase, low-angle action, first person, and fixed views of City Square, Kenyatta Avenue, Uhuru Park, Upper Hill and an aerial one. **F** switches to the free camera: click to look around (Esc releases the mouse) or drag, **WASD** moves, **Space**/**C** go up and down, **Shift** goes faster and the scroll wheel sets the speed. **Shift+1–9** fly it to the tallest towers and **Shift+0** back to the start view. **M** opens the street map, **H** hides the HUD. On a touch screen a joystick and buttons appear; a gamepad works too (left stick moves, right stick looks, the triggers drive or climb, the bumpers switch modes, Y the camera and Start the map).

The scene so far:
- **Terrain:** a mesh made from `GEO.terrain`. Parks, water, rivers, rail and building footprints are painted on a 4096 px map texture draped over it (`js/ground.js`). `heightAt` follows the mesh's own triangles, so whatever lies on the ground sits exactly on what is drawn.
- **Streets:** geometry on the terrain (`js/street-geometry.js`, `js/streets.js`). The carriageways are the traffic's own roads from the road network, as wide as it drives them, with junctions filled and their corners cut back, plus OSM's service roads. Every road but the motorways has a 4.2 m sidewalk along both sides, cut back wherever another carriageway crosses it, raised on a 15 cm kerb (where the pedestrians walk; the player steps up onto them). Footpaths are paved at grade. Every polygon is cut along the terrain's grid lines and cell diagonals, so each piece lies flat inside one terrain triangle. Road markings are Slop New York's road paint with Kenya's markings (`js/road-paint.js`): broken white centre and lane lines (solid on roads with two lanes or more each way), yellow edge lines on main roads, stop bars at signals, give-way lines at roundabouts and minor roads, and zebra stripes at OSM's crossings and the signalised crosswalks, on the Expressway's decks too. The asphalt, paving, kerb and paint materials are New York's (`js/tsl-materials.js`), dry.
- **Buildings:** every building and building part, extruded and standing on the lowest point of its footprint, with a parapet round its flat roof and a lift motor room on the bigger ones. A tower's parts share one footing (`js/buildings.js`). The facades are a shader (`createFacadeMaterial` in `js/tsl-materials.js`): windows on a grid of bays and storeys (OSM's `building:levels` where mapped), a taller shopfront ground floor, in three styles by size: painted render with punched windows and sills, concrete offices with piers and floor bands, and glass curtain walls with mullions and spandrels. Glass reflects the sky and the sun; at blue hour and night a share of the windows light up. Colours are Nairobi's: whites, creams and ochres, grey and sand concrete, blue-green and bronze glass, with OSM's `building:colour` where mapped. A captured 3D map model of the CBD (`tools/import-models.mjs`, from the untracked `Nairobi CBD Obj/` folder, georeferenced by matching its roofs to the OSM footprints) supplies a measured height for 1,421 buildings whose OSM height was an estimate (`MODEL_HEIGHTS`), and 7 landmark towers whose OSM outlines were wrong (`MODEL_BUILDINGS`, `js/nairobi-models.bin`). KICC (with its flared crown and the amphitheatre's cone) and CBK Pension Towers (twin sails under golden crown screens) are the capture's own triangles, cut out around their OSM parts and lined up with them, drawn two-sided because the capture winds them either way; their colliders are their OSM parts brought down to the captured roofs. Times Tower, I&M, the Hilton, Reinsurance Plaza and Pension Towers are remeshed, since the capture isn't clean geometry there (a roof slab per floor, walls only on the side it was seen from, roofs partly missing): each is rebuilt from a 0.5 m height map of its roofs, lined up with its OSM footprint, holes filled, its heights merged into levels and each level's outline traced into a prism (`earcut` triangulates the roofs), and those prisms replace its OSM outline as colliders and roofs. Towers the models cover badly, or whose fit is more than 13 m off, keep their OSM shapes. The landmark towers get their real looks, from reference photographs (`LANDMARKS` in `js/buildings.js`): Britam a pale glass prism, UAP Old Mutual, Prism, Bunge, KCB Plaza and the GTC towers light glass between white fins, Times Tower, I&M and Cooperative Bank House dark glass, KICC terracotta concrete ringed by its balconies, Teleposta, NSSF House and the Hilton pale concrete with ribbon windows, Nyayo House ochre concrete, Nation Centre white.
- **Elevated roads:** the Expressway and flyovers as decks on piers, 8 m per OSM `layer`. Slip roads ramp up from the street, and decks ramp down where they meet it (`js/bridges.js`, by the rule in `js/elevation.js` that the traffic drives by too).
- **Traffic:** about 200 cars (one in five a matatu: a Hiace minibus in loud colours with the yellow PSV band, driven a little harder), 45 boda bodas (riders in reflective vests, about half with a passenger, leaning into turns; `js/actor-models.js`) and 12 buses around the camera, driving on the left over the OSM road graph, with roundabouts (traffic on the ring has priority), signals at the mapped junctions and at generated ones on major crossings (`js/city-config.js`), and up the hills and over the Expressway (`js/road-network.js`, `js/traffic.js`, ported from Slop New York). Drivers pick turns at random but avoid streets they can see are jammed, and signals along a road are offset into green waves, so the CBD's short single-lane links don't fill on one light's red and waste the next one's green. A vehicle stuck for 90 s out of sight is placed somewhere else, so traffic around a parked camera doesn't end in gridlock.
- **People:** 340 pedestrians around the camera, walking the footpaths beside the streets and crossing at the signalised junctions on the WALK and at OSM's zebra crossings, with traffic giving way to them (a car that has stood 15 s at a zebra gets its turn). Now and then someone jaywalks: they cross mid-block wherever there is a gap, run the road part, and give up after 45 s if no gap comes (a driver waves them over after 20 s) (`js/pedestrian-system.js`, `js/pedestrian-routes.js`, `js/pedestrians.js`, ported from Slop New York). OSM maps no kerbs or sidewalks here, so `js/walk-grid.js` works out the ground on a 2 m grid: streets, rail and water divide it into curb blocks, buildings are obstacles, and the sidewalk is open ground within 7 m of a street.
- **Player:** walking, a car, a bicycle and a helicopter (`js/controllers.js`, ported from Slop New York), starting on Kenyatta Avenue. They move over the terrain and the Expressway decks, so the car can drive up a slip road, along the Expressway and under it; the car and the bicycle roll down hills and fall off whatever they leave the edge of, and the helicopter can land on the roofs (KICC's helipad works). Every building wall is a collider, as are the Expressway's parapets and piers (`js/colliders.js`: New York's colliders are boxes, Nairobi's footprints take any shape, so walls are segments). Traffic and pedestrians react to the player as in New York, on the Expressway too, and traffic stops for (and bumps into) the car, bicycle or person the player left parked. Where everyone was is saved in `localStorage` and restored on the next visit.
- **Light and shadows:** a hemisphere light, the sun with a 4096 px shadow map that follows the camera (sharp at street level, wider from the air), and a gradient sky, in four times of day (`js/lighting.js`). Buildings, the Expressway, vehicles and people cast shadows; the ground and streets receive them.
- **HUD:** Slop New York's, ported (`index.html`, `css/style.css`, `js/main.js`): the frame rate, a time-of-day switch (golden hour, midday, blue hour, night: `js/lighting.js` sets the sun, sky light, sky and fog; New York's physical sky and shadows come with Phase 5), teleports to nine landmarks, the mode switch with each mode's keys, a speed cluster (km/h, and altitude above sea level in the helicopter), the camera menu, and where you are as street-name signs under the place's name (`getLocation` in `js/city-config.js`). The radar and the full-screen street atlas (`js/nairobi-map.js`, from New York's `nyc-map.js`) draw the roads to their width, every building, parks, water, rivers, the railway and the Expressway, with street names, landmarks, search, and a click-to-teleport GPS route that follows the road graph (one-way streets only one way when driving). Touch and gamepad controls are New York's unchanged (`js/touch-controls.js`, `js/gamepad-controls.js`). New York's sound button and horn wait for the audio.
- **Cameras:** chase, action and first-person cameras that stay over the ground and come in front of any building between them and the player, fixed views, an aerial one and the free camera (`js/cameras.js`, `js/fly-camera.js`). `window.nairobi` exposes the scene, cameras, controllers, colliders, data, traffic and the street layout for debugging in the console. `nairobi.roof('times')` puts the active player (on foot, in the car, anything) on the roof of the tallest building whose name contains the text, `nairobi.roof(x, z)` on the building at that point, and `nairobi.roof(n)` on the nth tallest; walking and driving then carry on across roofs. The toolbar's Debug menu (the bug icon) has the same as a click: with **Teleport to a roof** on, clicking a building puts you on its roof (dragging still orbits the camera; Esc turns it off).

## Tests

```bash
npm test
```

Ports of Slop New York's controller and camera tests (`tests/`), run against Nairobi's colliders, plus checks for hills, ledges, decks, rooftop landings and the Nairobi cameras.

## Geodata

```bash
npm install                      # geotiff, for reading the elevation data
tools/fetch-geodata.sh           # OpenStreetMap via Overpass → tools/data/osm_*.json
node tools/fetch-terrain.mjs     # Copernicus GLO-30 elevation window → tools/data/dem_glo30.json
node tools/build-geodata.mjs     # → js/nairobi-geodata.js
```

- **Area:** `tools/bbox.json`. It covers the CBD, Uhuru Park and Central Park, the Railways, River Road, Ngara's southern edge and Upper Hill. Every `tools/data/q_*.overpassql` query uses the same box. If you change it, update the queries too.
- **Frame:** 1 unit = 1 m, x = east, z = south (north = -z), y = up. The origin is KICC. Kenya drives on the left (`GEO.frame.drivesOn`).
- **`GEO` contents:** `roads` (kind, lanes, one-way, width, bridge/tunnel/layer), `buildings` (footprint ring, height, `est: 1` when the height is estimated, `part: 1` for building parts), `areas` (parks, grass, water, parking…), `rivers`, `rail`, `stations`, `busStops`, `termini` (matatu/bus stations), `landmarks` (sorted tallest first), and street furniture: `signals`, `crossings`, `lamps`, `trees` and `treeRows`.
- **Terrain:** `GEO.terrain` is a 25 m grid of ground heights relative to KICC's ground, which is at 1,667 m (`base`). Heights in the box range from 1,638 m in the Nairobi River valley to 1,742 m on the Upper Hill and Kilimani ridges. The source is Copernicus GLO-30, a surface model that includes buildings and trees. The build filters it to bare ground: a ~60 m morphological opening plus light smoothing. Use `heightAt(x, z)` from `js/terrain.js` to sample it, and `baseOf(ring)` for a building's footing (the lowest point of its footprint). As a check, the Railway Station comes out at 1,661 m, the classic figure quoted for Nairobi's elevation.
- **Street furniture:** only what OSM maps, which is sparse in the CBD: 16 traffic signals, 93 crossings and 18 street lamps. Trees are better covered, with 395 trees and 42 tree rows. The sim will need to generate more of these itself.
- **Heights:** these come from OSM `height`, `building:levels` or `floors`. The towers (KICC, Times Tower, UAP Old Mutual, Britam, Teleposta, Rahimtulla…) are mapped as `building:part`s, and those parts replace their outlines. Small parts such as spires, crowns and facade fins are kept. If none of a building's parts start at ground level, the outline is kept as a podium (`podium: 1`) up to where the parts begin. Nation Centre's twin towers, for example, sit on a 10-storey base that is mapped only as the outline. About 95% of buildings have no height data in OSM. They get a fixed estimate based on size, use and distance from KICC.
- **Skyline check (Oct 2026):** checked against Wikipedia's *List of tallest buildings in Kenya*, which cites the CTBUH Skyscraper Center. Every listed tower inside the box is present and within about 6% of its listed height, except Britam, which is 195 m against the listed 200 m. Nation Centre is named "Nation Media House" in OSM; its two 83 m towers match the listed 82.9 m. Le'Mac and Sanlam Tower are in Westlands, outside the box.
